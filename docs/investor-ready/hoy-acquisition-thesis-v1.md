# HOY Acquisition Thesis v1.0

Status: **ACTIVE INTERNAL STRATEGY — NO BUYER-INTEREST CLAIM / NO OUTREACH AUTHORIZATION**

## Objective

HOY is not optimized merely to be technically acquirable. The objective is to increase **realizable strategic acquisition value** and the probability that multiple credible buyers could independently conclude that owning HOY creates more value for them than building an equivalent capability themselves.

The operating question is therefore:

> Does this work increase a credible buyer's willingness to pay, reduce the buyer's risk discount, or increase the number of credible buyers who could rationally compete for HOY?

If the answer is no, the work is not exit-priority before G3 unless it is required for safety, law, reliability or an already-approved market-proof experiment.

## Non-goals / claim boundary

- This thesis does **not** claim that any named company is interested in HOY.
- Named companies are adjacency examples or transaction precedents only.
- TheFork's announced $700m transaction is **not** a valuation comparable for HOY.
- Internal KPI triggers below are management thresholds, not market standards or sale guarantees.
- No investor, buyer, business or partner outreach is authorized while the existing HOY contact freeze remains active.
- No product feature receives priority merely because it sounds attractive in an acquisition narrative.

## Value equation

**Realizable acquisition value = validated standalone economics + buyer-specific synergy value - integration/risk discount + competitive-process premium.**

HOY can influence all four terms:

1. **Standalone economics** — paid business demand, recurring revenue, retention, gross margin, consumer repeat usage.
2. **Buyer-specific synergy** — merchant distribution, consumer intent, verified local supply, trip/dining/local-commerce cross-sell, regional launch engine, cross-vertical graph.
3. **Risk discount** — IP/data rights, privacy, security, transferability, founder independence, operating documentation.
4. **Competitive process** — several buyer classes must have a credible, different reason to own HOY.

## Buyer classes

### A. Dining networks / restaurant reservation and engagement platforms — Priority 1

**Adjacency examples:** American Express dining ecosystem (Resy/Tock and proposed TheFork acquisition), Booking Holdings / OpenTable.

**Why HOY could matter:** a dense, verified local restaurant supply graph; current availability/offer intent; merchant relationships; consumer discovery behavior; local operating playbook; a path to expand from discovery into attributed reservations or merchant demand generation without HOY needing to become a full reservation platform first.

**Buyer proof required:**
- first-party confirmed restaurant relationships and data;
- measurable consumer high-intent actions (reservation click, call, directions, offer interaction);
- repeat business value and willingness to pay;
- rights-safe merchant and provenance layer;
- evidence that a new region can be populated and operated repeatably.

**Do not build just for this buyer:** a full reservation stack without merchant/customer proof or a buyer/partner requirement.

### B. Local-commerce / delivery / merchant-demand platforms — Priority 1

**Adjacency examples:** Uber Eats / Uber Advertising; DoorDash + Deliveroo; Prosus + Just Eat Takeaway.com.

**Why HOY could matter:** merchant density plus local consumer intent can create demand-generation inventory, sponsored placement, offer activation, local discovery and cross-category commerce. HOY's value would be the verified local graph and demand signal, not another delivery fleet.

**Buyer proof required:**
- merchants pay for visibility, qualified leads, offers or measurable demand;
- high-intent local sessions can be attributed to merchant outcomes;
- business onboarding is cheaper/faster with every additional region;
- consumer intent signals are privacy-safe and commercially usable.

### C. Travel / full-trip platforms — Priority 1

**Adjacency examples:** Booking Holdings brands; Expedia Group.

**Why HOY could matter:** tourists need decisions after accommodation is booked. Gastro + Lifestyle can become a structured local decision layer covering food, activities, events and real-time availability. A travel platform could monetize more of the trip and improve local experience without building a region-by-region operating system itself.

**Buyer proof required:**
- meaningful tourist usage;
- cross-category sessions (e.g. Gastro → Lifestyle);
- measurable local-action conversion;
- evidence that HOY increases engagement or local spend opportunities during a trip;
- repeatable launch in multiple destination regions.

### D. Experiences / destination marketplaces — Priority 1

**Adjacency example:** Tripadvisor / Viator and comparable experience marketplaces.

**Why HOY could matter:** HOY Lifestyle plus Gastro can create a local intent layer around what to do now, events, activities and nearby food. The value rises if HOY can prove conversion from discovery to an attributable action without owning inventory.

**Buyer proof required:**
- Lifestyle demand and repeat usage;
- events/activities freshness;
- conversion to bookable or attributable actions;
- cross-sell between experience and restaurant intent.

### E. Hospitality merchant operating systems / POS — Priority 2

**Adjacency example:** Lightspeed Restaurant.

**Why HOY could matter:** merchant operating systems already own transaction/workflow relationships but may benefit from external demand generation, local discovery and verified public business data. HOY must prove incremental merchant value, not duplicate POS functionality.

**Buyer proof required:**
- merchants can quantify leads/revenue/traffic attributable to HOY;
- strong paid-business retention;
- low integration cost;
- merchant data confirmation and update workflow.

### F. Multi-vertical lifestyle-commerce / local marketplace groups — Priority 2

**Adjacency example:** Prosus ecosystem across food delivery and marketplaces.

**Why HOY could matter:** the strategic asset is the **regional operating system**, not one vertical. Gastro can establish consumer and merchant density; Lifestyle and Works can reuse identity, location, trust, business onboarding and regional operations. This becomes interesting only if cross-vertical reuse is measured rather than assumed.

**Buyer proof required:**
- same-region cross-vertical consumer usage;
- shared merchant/business acquisition efficiency;
- demonstrably lower launch cost for the second/third vertical;
- one operator can manage multiple verticals using common tooling.

### G. Local-data / location-intelligence buyers — Priority 2, research required

**Why HOY could matter:** structured, fresh, rights-governed, field-level provenance plus business confirmation can be more valuable than scraped listings. The defensibility is verification history and change/freshness operations, not raw public facts.

**Buyer proof required:**
- high first-party confirmation coverage;
- measurable freshness SLA;
- clear data-rights buckets and buyer-safe export;
- demonstrated use of changes/signals to improve a product or commercial outcome.

**Named buyer list:** intentionally deferred until current-market research identifies companies whose current product and M&A strategy genuinely fit. No speculative names are inserted for appearance.

### H. Financial buyers / SaaS or marketplace consolidators — Priority 3 until economics mature

**Why HOY could matter:** predictable recurring cashflow, transferable operations, low founder dependence and an expansion playbook.

**Buyer proof required:**
- recurring revenue and retention;
- healthy gross margin and CAC/payback;
- operating owner other than Jan;
- clean IP/contracts/data rights;
- credible multi-region growth path.

## Buyer-Pull evidence ladder

### BP0 — Acquisition Clean
G1 P0 risks are controlled; no critical IP/data/security/privacy/account-control blocker is hidden. This prevents a discount but does not create buyer pull by itself.

### BP1 — Paid Business Proof
At least one repeatable paid merchant proposition exists. Evidence means contracts/invoices/payments or equivalent auditable proof — not compliments or free pilots.

### BP2 — Consumer Demand Proof
HOY demonstrates real usage and repeat behavior. Vanity traffic does not count; high-intent actions and repeat usage matter.

### BP3 — Merchant Outcome Proof
HOY can attribute a useful outcome to the merchant: qualified lead, reservation handoff, call, direction request, offer redemption, traffic or another agreed measurable result.

### BP4 — Data / Supply Defensibility
Core business facts are increasingly first-party confirmed, freshness is operationally managed, and buyer-safe rights segregation exists.

### BP5 — Regional Repeatability
A second region launches using the same product and operating playbook without bespoke core-product rebuilding.

### BP6 — Founder Independence
A non-founder operator can run the region, business onboarding, QA and normal issue handling with bounded Jan involvement.

### BP7 — Quantified Buyer Synergy
For at least three buyer classes, HOY can model a buyer-specific value mechanism using real HOY data: incremental merchant reach, cross-sell, engagement, lower acquisition cost, faster regional entry or data/operations savings.

### BP8 — Competitive Buyer Process
Only after Contact Freeze is released and evidence thresholds are met: several qualified buyers are approached in parallel. One conversation is not a competitive process.

## Two execution lanes

### Lane F — Fast Strategic Option
Purpose: create an **early but credible option to sell well**, without prematurely giving away upside.

Working trigger to consider controlled buyer exploration (not a sale guarantee):
- no unresolved G1 P0 blocker that would prevent a transaction;
- at least 5 paying founding businesses;
- at least 15 total signed business proof points (paid + strong LOI/pilot commitments);
- measurable consumer usage with a first repeat-usage cohort;
- high-intent actions instrumented;
- buyer-safe data-rights segmentation working;
- at least two buyer classes have quantified synergy hypotheses based on real HOY data;
- founder-independent operating drill at least prepared and testable.

These are management triggers. They are recalibrated after the first real launch cohort.

### Lane V — Competitive Value Build
Purpose: maximize bargaining power and probability of multiple credible buyers.

Working trigger for a deliberate competitive process:
- at least 20 paying businesses;
- at least €3,000 monthly recurring B2B revenue or a stronger equivalent recurring commercial base;
- 90-day paid-business retention at or above 70% once the cohort is old enough to measure;
- at least 1,500 regional MAU with at least 30% 30-day repeat among activated consumers, or a stronger high-intent cohort metric;
- at least 60% of launch-critical core supply first-party confirmed;
- second-region launch proven without bespoke core rebuilding;
- non-founder operations tested;
- at least three buyer-specific synergy cases quantified;
- no unresolved deal-killing G1 issue.

Again: these are internal working thresholds, not external valuation rules.

## Buyer-Pull scorecard

No numeric Buyer-Pull Score is published until G2 evidence exists. When evidence exists, score 0–5 per dimension and retain the source for every score:

- paid revenue quality — 20%
- merchant retention/outcome — 15%
- consumer demand/repeat/high-intent actions — 15%
- supply density + first-party confirmation/data defensibility — 15%
- regional repeatability — 15%
- founder independence / transferability — 10%
- buyer-specific synergy evidence — 10%

A score without evidence is **UNKNOWN**, never zero and never inferred.

## Kill / deprioritize rules

Before G3, deprioritize work that:
- adds a feature without paid/usage/buyer-proof hypothesis;
- accumulates data without rights/freshness/verification value;
- expands geography before Region 1 has a reusable launch baseline;
- creates bespoke integrations without a signed commercial or strategic reason;
- optimizes vanity traffic instead of repeat/high-intent behavior;
- creates another governance layer instead of closing a proof/control;
- improves a buyer story but does not improve the underlying economics or evidence.

## Decision rule

A future HOY initiative is **EXIT-PRIORITY** only if its owner can name at least one measurable mechanism from this list:

- increases paid demand or recurring revenue;
- improves merchant outcome/retention;
- increases consumer repeat or high-intent actions;
- increases first-party confirmed / rights-governed data value;
- lowers launch cost/time for another region or vertical;
- lowers founder dependence or integration risk;
- strengthens a quantified buyer-specific synergy;
- increases the credible buyer universe or competitive-process leverage.

Otherwise it is normal product backlog, not acquisition-priority.
